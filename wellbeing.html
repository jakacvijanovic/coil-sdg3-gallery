Food & well-being

What can fit into a lunch box, a short break or a busy week? Choose a topic for practical ideas you can adapt at school or at home.

Build a satisfying lunch

Start with what you have

Combine a grain or potato with a protein food and some fruit or vegetables. Different foods bring different nutrients. Beans offer protein and fibre, while fruit and vegetables add variety in colour, flavour and texture.

Pack it tomorrow · Fill a wholegrain wrap with beans, grated carrot and yoghurt dressing. Or pack leftover rice with peas, egg or tofu, and an apple on the side. Choose ingredients that suit your budget and preferences.

Shop and cook on a budget

One ingredient, two meals

Frozen vegetables and canned pulses are convenient options. Compare similar products for added salt or sugar. Choose a few ingredients that work together so an opened pack can stretch across several meals.

Try it · Stir half a can of chickpeas into tomato soup. Refrigerate the rest promptly in a covered container and mash them with lemon for a sandwich filling the next day.

Choose drinks and snacks

Make the easy option appealing

Keep drinking water within reach. Choosing water more often in place of sugary drinks reduces free sugar intake. For a snack, try fruit with plain yoghurt or wholegrain toast with hummus.

At school · Find a place to refill your bottle. When comparing two drinks, check the sugar per 100 ml so different bottle sizes do not hide the difference.

Move, rest and reconnect

A break that fits your day

Walking, wheeling, dancing and active games all count as movement. Regular physical activity supports physical and mental health. Pick an activity you enjoy and adapt it to your space and ability.

Between lessons · Invite a friend for a short walk or stretch together. After a long day, set aside a quiet moment to read or talk. Ask yourself which break left you feeling more rested or connected.

Make a change together

Look beyond individual choices

A water fountain, an affordable school lunch and a safe place to spend a break give more people a chance to look after their health. That connects everyday routines to SDG 3.

Classroom challenge · Ask three classmates what would make lunch or break time easier. Pick one shared idea, test it for a week and compare what changed.
